# Coastal Lithuania — parameter guide and fallbacks

Companion to `coastal-lithuania-data-needs.md`. For every input the model reads
this lists: **what it is**, **units**, **where the real value comes from**, and a
**fallback** for when local data are missing. Fallbacks are ordered from best to
worst; the model runs with any of them, but calibration credibility degrades as
you go down each list.

A general principle: the model is more sensitive to **freshwater habitat,
hydraulics and recruitment** than to the marine constants, so spend data effort
top-down. Most bioenergetic constants can keep their inSTREAM/inSALMO literature
defaults — only flag them, don't block on them.

---

## 1. Spatial / per-cell habitat attributes (shapefile DBF)

Read via `spatial.gis_properties`. One row per habitat cell.

**`ID_TEXT`** — unique cell id (string). *Source:* auto-generated by the grid
builder. *Fallback:* none needed; always synthesised.

**`REACH_NAME`** — which reach the cell belongs to. *Source:* assigned when cells
are generated per river polygon. *Fallback:* none needed.

**`AREA`** — cell surface area (m²). *Source:* computed from polygon geometry.
*Fallback:* none needed; derived.

**`M_TO_ESC`** — distance from cell to nearest escape/hiding cover (m). Drives
terrestrial-predation risk (fish far from cover die more). *Source:* measured
cover positions from a habitat survey. *Fallback (1)* set proportional to distance-
to-bank from the geometry; *(2)* a single per-reach constant (e.g. 1–3 m for
small wooded streams, larger for open channels); *(3)* clone the Minija/Danė
values already in the fixture.

**`NUM_HIDING`** — count of hiding places (cobble/undercut/wood) in the cell.
Sets the cap on how many fish can shelter from predators. *Source:* substrate &
cover survey at electrofishing sites. *Fallback (1)* regression on substrate class
× area; *(2)* per-reach constant scaled by `AREA`; *(3)* clone fixture values.

**`FRACVSHL`** — fraction of the cell offering velocity shelter (0–1), i.e. slow
microhabitat behind cobble/wood where a fish spends less energy holding station.
*Source:* hydraulic/habitat survey. *Fallback (1)* tie to substrate roughness or
the reach `shelter_speed_frac`; *(2)* per-reach constant (high-gradient riffle
streams ~0.2–0.3, lowland glides lower); *(3)* clone fixture.

**`FRACSPWN`** — fraction of the cell that is suitable spawning gravel (0–1).
**The most decision-relevant trout habitat field** — controls where redds can be
placed. *Source:* substrate mapping / spawning-gravel survey (redd-count studies
already locate these in Danė/Šventoji tributaries). *Fallback (1)* set >0 only in
known spawning reaches from the literature, 0 elsewhere; *(2)* assign a small
constant (e.g. 0.05–0.15) to riffle-classified cells; *(3)* clone fixture — least
defensible because spawning location structure is lost.

## 2. Per-reach environmental & food parameters (`reaches:` block)

**`pspc_smolts_per_year`** — potential smolt production capacity, the recruitment
ceiling for the reach (smolts/yr). *Source:* **smolt-trap counts** + HELCOM/ICES
WGBAST river tables for LT sea trout. *Fallback (1)* habitat area × literature
smolt density (smolts·m⁻²·yr⁻¹ for Baltic sea trout, ~0.1–1); *(2)* scale the
existing order-of-magnitude Nemunas estimates by relative river length; *(3)*
leave unset (`None`) — disables the PSPC cap, recruitment then purely emergent.

**`drift_conc`** — drifting-invertebrate concentration, the main food supply
(kg·m⁻³, very small ~1e-8). Strongly affects growth. *Source:* benthic/drift
invertebrate sampling. *Fallback (1)* transfer values from a similar boreal
lowland stream in the literature; *(2)* use the fixture's gradient (richer
headwaters → poorer lagoon/sea); *(3)* treat as a **calibration free parameter**
(the calibration framework already targets `drift_conc`) — recommended when no
drift data exist.

**`search_prod`** — search-feeding productivity, the alternative (non-drift) food
pathway. *Source:* same invertebrate sampling. *Fallback:* same as `drift_conc` —
scale together, or calibrate.

**`prey_energy_density`** — energy content of prey (J·g⁻¹). *Source:* prey
bomb-calorimetry / literature. *Fallback:* keep literature defaults (aquatic
inverts ~3000–4500 J·g⁻¹; the fixture already grades these); rarely worth measuring.

**`drift_regen_distance`** — upstream distance over which drift food regenerates
(m). *Source:* hard to measure directly. *Fallback:* keep inSTREAM-style values
(hundreds of m in productive streams, larger in lakes/sea); calibrate if needed.

**`shelter_speed_frac`** — reach-level velocity-shelter effectiveness (fraction).
*Source:* hydraulic survey. *Fallback:* per-reach constant by channel type (see
`FRACVSHL` above); clone fixture.

**`shading`** — riparian shading fraction (0–1); moderates light and temperature
effects. *Source:* canopy cover from orthophotos/lidar or field. *Fallback (1)*
derive from a land-cover/canopy raster at geoportal.lt; *(2)* per-reach constant
(forested headwater high ~0.7–0.8, open lowland/urban low); *(3)* clone fixture.

**`fish_pred_min`** / **`terr_pred_min`** — minimum daily survival from fish and
terrestrial predation in the reach (the survival floor at worst exposure).
*Source:* predator survey (pike/perch density; heron/cormorant/otter). *Fallback
(1)* set from predator-presence categories (lagoon/sea low survival, headwater
high); *(2)* clone fixture gradient; *(3)* calibrate against parr survival. For
coastal LT, **cormorant pressure** is high and not separately modelled — fold it
into `fish_pred_min`/`terr_pred_min` until a dedicated forcing exists.

**`light_turbid_coef`**, **`light_turbid_const`** — turbidity→light attenuation
coefficients affecting feeding and predation. *Source:* paired turbidity/light
measurement. *Fallback:* keep fixture/inSTREAM values; these are second-order.

**`max_spawn_flow`** — discharge above which spawning is suppressed (model flow
units). *Source:* local flow at observed spawning. *Fallback:* set from the reach's
flow regime (≈ mean-to-moderate-high daily flow); large sentinel (999) in
non-spawning marine reaches.

**`shear_A`, `shear_B`** — bed-shear / stranding-risk coefficients linking flow
change to dewatering mortality. *Source:* hydraulic model. *Fallback:* keep
inSTREAM defaults graded by channel size (fixture already does this); calibrate if
stranding matters.

**`upstream_junction`, `downstream_junction`** — integer node ids defining how
reaches connect into the network. *Source:* the river topology itself (which reach
flows into which). *Fallback:* none — you must set these by hand from a map; they
are structure, not measured data.

**`time_series_input_file`, `depth_file`, `velocity_file`** — filenames pointing to
the CSVs in §3–4. Not data themselves.

## 3. Hydraulic rating tables (`<Reach>-Depths.csv`, `<Reach>-Vels.csv`)

A table of **per-cell depth (m) and velocity (m·s⁻¹) at N discharge levels**
(fixture uses 10 flows). The model interpolates cell depth/velocity from the day's
flow. *Source:* surveyed cross-sections + 1-D/2-D hydraulic model (HEC-RAS), or
gauged depth–velocity ratings, draped on the national lidar DEM. *Fallback (1)*
build a simple hydraulic-geometry relation (depth ∝ Q^b, velocity ∝ Q^f with
literature exponents) from a single measured cross-section per reach; *(2)* the
current synthetic approach — per-cell jitter around a **published mean depth/
velocity** scaled by flow (documented as synthetic, acceptable for relative
scenarios and calibration but not absolute habitat area). At minimum you need one
real **mean depth and mean velocity** per reach to anchor the table.

---

## 4. Daily environmental time series (`<Reach>-TimeSeriesInputs.csv`)

Columns `Date,temperature,flow,turbidity`, one row per simulation day.

**`temperature`** — daily mean water temperature (°C). The single strongest driver
(bioenergetics + every temperature-threshold mortality). *Source:* **LHMT / AAA**
water-temperature records. *Fallback (1)* regress water temp on air temp
(LHMT/ERA5) where overlapping data exist, then hindcast; *(2)* a seasonal model
(sinusoid fit to any spot measurements); *(3)* the current synthetic sinusoid —
use only as placeholder.

**`flow`** — daily discharge (m³·s⁻¹). Drives depth/velocity lookup, stranding,
spawning suppression. *Source:* **LHMT gauges** (Danė, Šventoji, Bartuva, Minija
all gauged). *Fallback (1)* transfer from a nearby gauged catchment by drainage-
area ratio; *(2)* a rainfall-runoff model (GR4J/HBV) from LHMT precipitation; *(3)*
synthetic seasonal series.

**`turbidity`** — daily turbidity (NTU). Modulates feeding and predation via the
light/turbid terms. *Source:* **AAA** surface-water monitoring (usually sparse).
*Fallback (1)* turbidity–flow rating (turbidity rises with flow) fit to any spot
samples; *(2)* derive from TSS if only TSS exists; *(3)* low constant (clear small
streams) — turbidity is the least sensitive of the three, so a constant is
tolerable.

## 5. Initial population (`*-InitialPopulations.csv`)

Columns: `Species, Reach, Age, Number, Length min, Length mode, Length max`.

**`Number`** per reach × age — starting abundance. *Source:* **0+/1+ parr
densities from LT electrofishing** × wetted habitat area. *Fallback (1)* literature
Baltic sea-trout parr densities × area; *(2)* scale the fixture's age structure to
your reach areas; *(3)* seed a nominal cohort and discard a multi-year spin-up
("burn-in") so the starting numbers stop mattering — **recommended fallback**, since
the model converges away from initial conditions.

**`Length min/mode/max`** per age — triangular length distribution at age. *Source:*
length-frequency from electrofishing. *Fallback:* keep fixture values (they're
generic salmonid age-length) or back out from a von Bertalanffy curve for LT sea
trout; low sensitivity after spin-up.

---

## 6. Adult returns (`*-AdultArrivals.csv`)

Columns: `Year, Species, Reach, Number, Fraction female, Arrival start/peak/end,
Length min/mode/max`.

**`Number`** — returning spawners per reach per year. *Source:* redd counts, trap/
weir counts. *Fallback (1)* derive from smolt output × an assumed marine survival
(SAR); *(2)* let returns be **fully emergent** from the simulated marine pipeline
and don't force external arrivals at all (drop the file) — cleanest if you trust
the lifecycle, avoids inventing numbers.

**`Fraction female`** — sex ratio of returners. *Source:* catch/survey sexing.
*Fallback:* 0.5 (or the ~0.55 in the fixture).

**`Arrival start/peak/end`** — run-timing dates. *Source:* trap/redd phenology for
LT sea trout (autumn-weighted, differs from salmon). *Fallback:* shift to a
sea-trout autumn run window from HELCOM RETROUT; widen the window if uncertain.

**`Length min/mode/max`** — returner size. *Source:* catch length data. *Fallback:*
sea-trout sea-age length-at-return from literature; fixture salmon sizes are too
large for most LT coastal trout.

## 7. Species biological parameters (`species:` block)

Split into "must localise for sea trout" vs "keep literature defaults".

### 7a. Should be sea-trout-specific (localise)

**`weight_A`, `weight_B`** — length-weight power law W = A·Lᴮ (cm→g). *Source:* LT
sea-trout L-W regression from survey weights. *Fallback:* FishBase *Salmo trutta*
L-W; or keep salmon values (close but biased).

**`spawn_fecund_mult`, `spawn_fecund_exp`** — fecundity = mult·Lᵉˣᵖ (eggs per
female by length). *Source:* egg counts vs length from LT spawners. *Fallback:*
*Salmo trutta* fecundity–length from literature; smaller trout produce fewer eggs
than the salmon defaults, so this matters.

**`spawn_start_day`, `spawn_end_day`** — spawning window. *Source:* LT redd-count
phenology (sea trout spawn late autumn, ~Oct–Dec). *Fallback:* generic Baltic
sea-trout window; do **not** keep the salmon window.

**`emerge_length_min/mode/max`** — fry length at emergence (cm). *Source:* early
fry sampling. *Fallback:* *Salmo trutta* emergence length from literature (~2.5–3 cm).

**`outmigration_min_length`, `smolt_min_length`** (marine, §8) — smolt-size
threshold. *Source:* LT smolt-trap lengths. *Fallback:* Baltic sea-trout smolt
~12–18 cm (larger than salmon); set from HELCOM BSEP 126B LT chapter.

**`spawn_max_temp`, `spawn_min_temp`** — temperature window for spawning. *Source:*
observed spawning temps. *Fallback:* salmonid defaults (≈5–14 °C) are fine.

### 7b. Keep literature/inSTREAM defaults (flag, don't block)

These come from the inSTREAM/inSALMO trout parameterisation and are not normally
re-measured per river. Keep the defaults and note them as assumptions:

- **Consumption:** `cmax_A`, `cmax_B`, `cmax_temp_table` (Wisconsin max-consumption
  scaling + temperature-dependence curve).
- **Respiration:** `resp_A`, `resp_B`, `resp_C`, `resp_D`, `resp_ref_temp`.
- **Energy:** `energy_density` (fish J·g⁻¹), `prey_energy_density` (per reach).
- **Swimming/movement:** `max_speed_A..E`, `move_radius_*`, `search_area`,
  `react_dist_A/B`, `move_radius_max`.
- **Vision/feeding modifiers:** `turbid_threshold/min/exp`, `light_threshold/min/exp`.
- **Fitness behaviour:** `fitness_horizon`, `fitness_length`, `fitness_memory_frac`,
  `fitness_growth_weight`, `migrate_fitness_L1/L9`.
- **Mortality response shapes:** `mort_high_temp_T1/T9`, `mort_strand_survival_when_dry`,
  `mort_condition_S_at_K5/K8`, all `mort_terr_pred_*` and `mort_fish_pred_*`
  logistic breakpoints, `mort_redd_*` (dewater/scour/temp).
- **Redd development:** `redd_devel_A/B/C` (temperature-dependent egg development),
  `redd_area`, `spawn_egg_viability`.
- **Spawning rule thresholds:** `spawn_min_age/length/cond` (−9 = "disabled"),
  `spawn_prob`, `spawn_wt_loss_fraction`, `spawn_suitability_tol`,
  `spawn_max_flow_change`, `spawn_defense_area(_m)`, `spawn_depth_table`,
  `spawn_vel_table`.
- **Super-individual bookkeeping:** `superind_max_rep`, `superind_max_length`,
  `fecundity_noise`, `spawn_date_jitter_days`.
- **Kelt survival:** `kelt_survival_prob`, `min_kelt_condition` — sea trout are
  more iteroparous than salmon, so if any LT repeat-spawning data exist, raise
  these; otherwise defaults are a reasonable fallback.
- **Capture/angling:** `capture_R1`, `capture_R9` — only matter if you enable the
  harvest module; otherwise leave as-is.

**General fallback for any 7b parameter:** keep the value, expose it as a
calibration free parameter only if a sensitivity screen (the built-in Morris/Sobol
`preflight_screen`) flags it as influential for your target outputs. Don't hunt for
data on parameters the model isn't sensitive to.

## 8. Marine domain (`marine:` block)

**`zones` / `area_km2`** — Estuary/Coastal/Baltic-Proper sizes. *Source:* defined by
your study geography. *Fallback:* keep abstract areas; sea trout stay coastal, so
weight toward the Estuary/Coastal zones.

**`static_driver` monthly `temperature`, `salinity`** — SE Baltic climatology.
*Source:* Copernicus Marine / HELCOM / LHMT Klaipėda coastal station. *Fallback:*
keep the fixture's monthly arrays (already SE-Baltic-plausible ~7 PSU); refine later.

**`prey_index`, `predation_risk`** (monthly, per zone) — relative marine food and
risk. *Source:* hard to observe directly. *Fallback:* keep fixture seasonal
shapes; treat as calibration knobs against marine-survival targets.

**`smolt_min_length`, `return_min_sea_winters`** — life-history gates. *Source:* LT
smolt/return age data. *Fallback:* sea-trout values (smaller sea-winter counts,
larger smolt length than salmon) from HELCOM RETROUT.

**`post_smolt_survival_forcing_csv`** (Arc N) — per-year marine survival. *Source:*
WGBAST/HELCOM. *Fallback (1)* a constant SAR from sea-trout literature; *(2)*
calibrate against adult-return targets.

**`seal_abundance_csv`** (Arc P) — HELCOM grey-seal abundance scaling marine
predation. *Source:* HELCOM seal assessments. *Fallback:* disable (omit the key —
defaults to no seal scaling); add later since coastal trout are seal/cormorant
exposed.

**`stock_unit`, `stray_fraction`, `m74_forcing_csv`** — WGBAST coupling knobs.
*Source:* assessment data. *Fallback:* leave at None/0 defaults — they are opt-in
and a run without them preserves baseline behaviour.

## How to use this when data are thin

A workable **minimum-data coastal-LT build**:

1. Real geometry (OSM) + one real **mean depth/velocity** per reach → §1, §3.
2. Real **flow + water temperature** from LHMT → §4 (biggest realism lever).
3. Sea-trout **species block** for §7a; keep §7b defaults.
4. Everything else: literature transfer or **expose as calibration free
   parameters** and fit against whatever observation series exist (parr density,
   smolt counts). The calibration framework + Morris/Sobol screen tells you which
   unknowns actually matter, so you only chase data that changes the answer.
