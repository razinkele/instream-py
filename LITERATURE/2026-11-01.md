### Peer-reviewed

**[P1]** Foldvik, A. (2026). *Natural freshwater mortality rates in pre-spawning adult Atlantic salmon, a systematic review using data from telemetry studies.* *Fisheries Research*, 2026. [DOI not confirmed; identified via Consensus — verify before citing] <https://ezproxy.biblioteka.ku.lt/login?url=https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165783626XXXX> *(proxy link pending DOI confirmation)*  
> Systematic review of telemetry studies (Web of Science search to 30 September 2025; 34 studies retained from 541 records; 29 rivers; 37 river–year survival estimates) of pre-spawning adult *Salmo salar* freshwater mortality, excluding fishing mortality. Mean instantaneous mortality rate Z = 0.43; mean annualised survival = 0.80; majority of individual river estimates (21 of 37) showed zero or negligible mortality. A sharp increase in mortality was associated with temperatures above 21°C and/or high predation. Freshwater survival was consistently higher than post-kelt ocean survival from the same river systems. Published 2026.  
> **instream-py implication:** InSTREAM/InSALMO focuses on juveniles, but the egg-deposition initialization for each cohort depends on spawner abundance reaching the reach. Foldvik's empirically grounded mean annualised pre-spawning survival (~80%) and the 21°C thermal mortality threshold give quantitative anchors for (a) the spawning-adult arrival probability when building the Baltic config's annual-cohort initialization, and (b) calibrating the temperature mortality sub-model specifically for *S. salar* adults — distinct from the current Californian trout (brook/rainbow trout) parameterization. *Baltic relevance: medium (multi-river synthesis, includes Nordic rivers; 21°C threshold relevant under projected Baltic summer warming). Touches: temperature mortality sub-model, spawning adult parameterization, Baltic baseline initialization.*

**[P2 — contextual, Pacific salmonid]** Keefer, M., et al. (2026). *Migration phenology of adult Chinook salmon: tradeoffs among acute and cumulative thermal exposure risks.* *Journal of Thermal Biology*, 2026. Copyright © 2026 Elsevier Ltd. [DOI not confirmed; identified via Consensus]  
> Agent-based model (ABM) of 161 radio-tagged adult spring-run Chinook (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) migrating 220 km through Oregon's Willamette/Santiam system. Movement rules derived empirically; cumulative degree-day (DD) exposure tracked through migration + holding. Mean total DD accumulation ~1100 (cool year) to ~1500 (warm year). ~3–24% of simulated salmon encountered temperatures ≥21°C (the known behavioral/physiological threshold). Prespawn mortality from carcass data 29% (cool) vs 61% (warm year). ABM can quantify cumulative vs acute thermal risk.  
> **instream-py implication:** Not directly applicable (Chinook, Pacific, Oregon). The cumulative-thermal-exposure ABM framing is methodologically interesting if instream-py is ever extended to model adult upstream migration in Baltic rivers under climate warming scenarios. The 21°C threshold is consistent with Foldvik (2026) for *S. salar*. Noted as conceptual; no issue opened. *Baltic relevance: low. Conceptual / future extension only.*

## Implications for inSTREAM

1. **Temperature mortality sub-model needs Atlantic salmon–specific adult threshold.** Foldvik (2026) synthesizes 37 river–year survival estimates and confirms a sharp pre-spawning adult mortality increase above 21°C in *Salmo salar*. The current instream-py codebase inherits InSTREAM's Californian trout parameterization for the temperature mortality sub-model. When building the Baltic config, the 21°C lethal threshold should be verified against the *S. salar*–specific literature (Foldvik 2026 being the most comprehensive current synthesis) rather than assumed equivalent to trout.

2. **Spawning-adult survival as Baltic initialization input.** Foldvik's mean annualized freshwater survival of ~80% (range: 0–100% by study) provides a defensible default for the probability that a Baltic river spawner reaches the redd rather than dying en route. This is a useful prior when data on a specific Baltic river are sparse.

3. **InSALMO repository status unclear.** The SFJohnston/inSALMO GitHub returned 404 this month; it is worth confirming whether the repository has moved, been made private, or been archived. If the code is no longer publicly accessible, this affects instream-py's ability to track upstream model evolution.

This is a code annotation + parameter audit task, not a new sub-model.
