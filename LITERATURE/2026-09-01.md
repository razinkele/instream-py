### Peer-reviewed — Late discovery (published August 2025; missed by the June–July 2026 scan)

**[P1]** Hajiesmaeili, M. (2025). *Scaling Up Individual-Based Models for Salmonid Populations in Hydropower-Regulated Rivers: Key Advances and Challenges.* *River Research and Applications*, online first. <https://doi.org/10.1002/rra.70023>  
> First large-scale application of inSALMO 7.3-SD (the model parent of instream-py) to three key spawning and rearing reaches in River Gullspång, Sweden — the same river and paper context targeted by open issue #1. Model covers 1.1 km, 0.105 km² for Atlantic salmon and brown trout. Main finding: flow management effects on outmigrant production are **complex and non-linear** — no flow scenario increased trout outmigrants; higher flows only improved age-2 salmon outmigrants in specific reaches; reach-level and species-specific variation is large. Spawning redd locations are validated against field observations.  
> **instream-py implication:** The model-predicted outmigrant numbers (by species, age class, flow scenario) and validated redd distributions are directly usable as quantitative validation targets for instream-py's forthcoming Gullspång/Baltic baseline config (issue #1). The reach-specific flow results also caution against assuming a uniform flow benefit for Baltic rivers: the IBM must be run at reach resolution to capture these interactions. *Baltic relevance: high (Gullspång, Baltic-catchment salmon & trout). Touches: IBM scaling, smolt production, flow–habitat interaction, multi-reach parameterization, validation targets.* **OA (cc-by).**

## Implications for inSTREAM

1. The Hajiesmaeili (2025) Gullspång results provide the first publicly available, species- and reach-specific inSALMO outmigrant predictions for the exact system targeted by issue #1. Extracting these as numeric validation targets (salmon and trout outmigrants per reach per flow scenario) should be done before or alongside the Baltic config work, not after.
2. The non-linear flow–production relationship (higher minimum flows *reduced* trout outmigrants in all three reaches) is a direct warning against assuming that higher-flow restoration scenarios will uniformly benefit salmonids in Baltic rivers. The instream-py Baltic config must capture this reach-level heterogeneity.
3. No new ICES or HELCOM resources were found this month; the WGBAST 2026 advice, once published, remains the primary external validation target for smolt production targets (see implications from the August 2026 digest).
