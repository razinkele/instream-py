## Items this month

### Peer-reviewed

**[P1]** Berry, M.H., Austad, B., & Aarestrup, K. (2026). *Smolt migration in relation to latitude, season and migratory distance.* openRxiv (preprint). <https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.20.733518>  
> PIT-tagged sea trout smolts across five European watersheds (Norway → Portugal). Spring migration timing differs significantly between watersheds with a trend of earlier migration at lower latitudes. Within-stream origin (upstream vs downstream) predicts autumn vs spring emigration; body size predicts spring migration date in Sweden. High mortality noted at sea-entry.  
> **instream-py implication:** Directly relevant to the smolt downstream-migration sub-model. Individual body size and within-stream spatial position are both trackable in the IBM; hardcoding a single fixed or temperature-only emigration trigger omits these significant drivers. Baltic latitudes (Lithuania, ~56 °N) sit between the Swedish and Danish study watersheds; timing parameters from Nordic sites are the closest available analogue. *Touches: smolt migration timing, diel behaviour, downstream mortality.*

**[P2]** McNeely, K.S., Sellheim, K., & Ross, K. (2026). *ESHE: A Life-Cycle–Integrated Modeling Framework for Quantifying Salmon Rearing Habitat.* Springer/preprint. <https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9360842/v1>  
> Emigrating Salmonid Habitat Estimator (ESHE) simulates emergence, rearing, and outmigration of Chinook cohorts on a daily timestep with size-dependent growth, migration rates, and territory requirements. Sensitivity analysis identifies survival parameters as the **strongest driver** of model outputs — larger effect than flow or habitat extent.  
> **instream-py implication:** Independent confirmation that survival sub-models dominate IBM output variance. Motivates a formal sensitivity/uncertainty analysis of instream-py's freshwater survival parameters before Baltic calibration. Chinook-focused, so not a Baltic parameterization source, but framework is directly transferable. *Touches: juvenile rearing survival, sensitivity analysis, habitat-cell suitability.*

**[P3]** Sorel, M.H., Zabel, R.W., & Murdoch, A.R. (2026). *Management strategy evaluation for salmon habitat restoration and hatchery supplementation.* *Conservation Science and Practice*, 8(6). <https://doi.org/10.1111/csp2.70309>  
> Density-dependent juvenile survival model for endangered Chinook; >150 % increase in juvenile survival required to meet recovery targets; marginal benefit of hatchery fish declines as wild survival improves.  
> **instream-py implication:** Consistent with [P2] on survival parameter primacy. Hatchery-supplementation modelling (highly relevant to Baltic rivers with stocking programmes including Nemunas) depends critically on density-dependent survival parameterization. *Touches: juvenile survival, density dependence, stocking scenarios.* Conceptual/validation; not directly actionable for Baltic numbers without local data.

**[P4]** Lamarins, A., Waples, R.S., & Piironen, J. (2026). *Extremely low effective population size in a captive-bred population: partial mitigation through management practices.* openRxiv (preprint). <https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.12.724519>  
> Saimaa salmon (*Salmo salar*, landlocked, critically endangered) long-term demographic data 1969–2024. Ne = 33–81 individuals (32–75 % of census size). Captive breeding practices increased Ne by ~12 %; post-early-stage survival variance (beyond management control) remains the key determinant.  
> **instream-py implication:** Saimaa salmon is the closest well-studied Baltic Atlantic salmon population to Lithuanian rivers. Long-term survival and reproductive-success data could serve as validation anchors. Post-early-stage survival variance finding directly motivates improved stochastic survival parameterization in the IBM. *Baltic relevance: high. Touches: survival, spawning, population dynamics.* Non-actionable for direct IBM parameterization without raw data, but flags validation opportunity.

**[P5]** Flávio, H., Sortland, L.K., & Birnie-Gauvin, K. (2026). *High Levels of Straying in Returning, Repeat-Spawning Atlantic Salmon Between Two Neighbouring Danish Rivers.* *Ecology and Evolution*, 16(7). <https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.73997>  
> Atlantic salmon kelts (repeat spawners) from River Skjern, Denmark: 70 % of returning fish strayed to a neighbouring river; hatchery kelts show 0 % repeat spawning vs 6.5 % for wild fish; kelt survival to sea 65.6 % (wild) vs 20 % (hatchery post-strip).  
> **instream-py implication:** Iteroparity and post-spawn survival are not currently modelled in instream-py. Interesting for multi-year simulation scenarios but not immediately actionable for current IBM scope. *Non-actionable for current development; note for future.*

**[P6]** Fullerton, A.H., Goodman, A., & Jorgensen, J.C. (2026). *Predicted Stream Temperatures Suggest Challenges for Pacific Salmon in Coming Decades.* *JAWRA*, 62(3). <https://doi.org/10.1111/1752-1688.70137>  
> Stream temperature rise ~1 °C by 2050s, ~2 °C by 2080s in Pacific NW USA. Pre-spawn mortality increases at warming sites. Thermal refuges persist in glaciated/high-elevation areas.  
> **instream-py implication:** Conceptually relevant to the temperature-mortality sub-model. Pacific/US-centric; Baltic-specific stream temperature projections are needed before this translates to actionable parameterization. *Low priority; not actionable now.*

## Implications for inSTREAM

1. The smolt emigration trigger in instream-py should incorporate individual body size and within-stream position alongside temperature cues (P1). The current Californian parameterization almost certainly uses fixed or temperature-only thresholds inappropriate for Baltic latitudes.
2. Survival sub-models are the highest-leverage parameters in IBM outputs (P2, P3). Prioritizing a structured sensitivity analysis before Baltic calibration would identify which survival parameters most need field data.
3. Saimaa salmon demographic data (P4) represents the most accessible Baltic-adjacent long-run validation dataset for *Salmo salar* survival and reproduction. A literature-mining effort targeted at Saimaa monitoring publications could yield calibration anchors.
4. No InSTREAM/InSALMO model updates were released this month; instream-py development is not impacted by upstream changes.

## Roadmap (inSTREAM)

### High

### Medium

**M1. Implement size- and within-stream-position dependent smolt emigration timing**  
*Motivated by:* Berry et al. 2026 (10.64898/2026.06.20.733518)  
*Effort:* Medium  
Body size at emigration and within-stream origin (upstream/downstream position) both significantly predict emigration timing in European sea trout. Both quantities are tracked per-individual in the IBM. Adding a position × size modifier to the emigration-trigger function would bring the model into alignment with observed Nordic patterns and improve smolt production estimates for Baltic tributaries.

**M2. Add structured sensitivity analysis for juvenile freshwater survival parameters**  
*Motivated by:* McNeely et al. 2026 (10.21203/rs.3.rs-9360842/v1); Sorel et al. 2026 (10.1111/csp2.70309)  
*Effort:* Medium  
Two independent modelling studies this month identify survival parameters as dominant drivers of IBM output variance. Before Baltic calibration (issue #1 target), a one-at-a-time or Morris screening sensitivity analysis of instream-py's temperature, predation, and stranding mortality parameters would identify the highest-leverage uncertainties and focus field-data collection efforts.

### Low

**L1. Explore Saimaa salmon demographic dataset as Baltic validation anchor**  
*Motivated by:* Lamarins et al. 2026 (10.64898/2026.05.12.724519)  
*Effort:* Low (literature mining)  
The Saimaa salmon captive-breeding dataset (Ne, survival variance) is published and openly accessible. Mapping it against instream-py's population-scale outputs could provide a first Baltic-adjacent validation point without requiring new field data.

**L2. Flag temperature-mortality escalation as climate-projection extension**  
*Motivated by:* Fullerton et al. 2026 (10.1111/1752-1688.70137)  
*Effort:* Low (scoping only until Baltic temperature projections are compiled)  
Not currently actionable; add to backlog for when Baltic stream temperature projections are available.
