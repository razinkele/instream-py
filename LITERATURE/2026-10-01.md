## Items this month

### Peer-reviewed

**[P1]** Tamario, C., Hauber, M. M., van Toor, M. L., et al. (2026). *Severe thiamine deficiency in Baltic salmon coincides with low wild recruitment, increasingly so at long migration distances.* *Biology Letters*, 22(9). <https://ezproxy.biblioteka.ku.lt/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2026.0219>  
> Combines a 30+ year M74 thiamine-deficiency monitoring dataset (eight Baltic Atlantic salmon rivers) with electrofishing-based wild recruitment estimates. Severe M74 coincides with lower wild recruitment, and the negative association steepens with longer within-river migration distance. Effect absent in Atlantic-coast salmon, pointing to a Baltic/marine-origin factor. Published 2026-09-09.  
> **instream-py implication:** When validating the Baltic baseline config (issue #1) against WGBAST wild-smolt production estimates, years with high M74 incidence should be flagged as external-mortality co-variates: the freshwater IBM cannot predict M74-driven early mortality, so low-recruitment years that are M74-linked represent conditions outside the model's scope and should not be used to tune freshwater survival parameters. *Baltic relevance: high (directly about Baltic salmon rivers). Touches: Baltic baseline validation pipeline, external-mortality accounting, smolt production targets.*

**[P2]** Lennox, R. J., Vollset, K. W., Berhe, S., et al. (2026). *Predation Risk and Depth Use During Migration of Atlantic Salmon Smolts Through a Fjord Impacted by Hydropower Discharge.* *Ecology of Freshwater Fish*, 35(4). <https://ezproxy.biblioteka.ku.lt/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1111/eff.70062>  
> Acoustic-telemetry study of smolt outmigration through River Suldalslågen (Norway) and its fjord under hydropower-regulated flows (2023–2024). Smolt mortality concentrates near the river mouth and one fjord bottleneck (bridge). Predation sensors confirm aquatic predators (brown trout + marine teleosts) cause a measurable share of fjord mortality. Diel depth-use pattern documented: smolts move deeper during daytime. Hydropower freshets shift route selection in the fjord. Published 2026-09-09.  
> **instream-py implication:** The river-mouth and bottleneck predation pattern is consistent with the IBM's existing reach-level predation formulation; the Lennox et al. data would be a useful empirical anchor for any post-river (estuarine/lagoon) mortality parameter in a Baltic life-cycle extension. The diel depth result corroborates four-timestep diel behaviour for smolts during outmigration. Currently *out of scope* for the freshwater IBM, but relevant for a future Curonian Lagoon marine-phase extension. *Baltic relevance: medium (Norway fjord, hydropower-regulated; analogous to Baltic river mouths). Touches: predation sub-model, diel behaviour, post-river mortality.* Closed-access; KU proxy required.

**[P3]** Kärkkäinen, E., Kankare, V., Erkinaro, J., et al. (2026). *Seasonal Dynamics of Instream Mesohabitats Over a Long Section of a Large Subarctic River.* *Ecohydrology*, 19(5). <https://ezproxy.biblioteka.ku.lt/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1002/eco.70268>  
> Maps mesohabitat types at 1 m xy resolution along 53 km of the Tana River (subarctic; Atlantic salmon) across nine discharge scenarios derived from a 66-year daily flow record, using multispectral ALS + 2D hydraulic modelling + the Norwegian Mesohabitat Classification Method. Deep glides, pools, and rapids dominate; post-spring-flood contraction dries many spring mesohabitats; summer-to-autumn transitions are more stable. Published 2026-01-01 (newly appearing in search results).  
> **instream-py implication:** The multi-discharge habitat-cell mapping approach—especially the quantification of seasonal mesohabitat contraction and expansion—is directly applicable to how instream-py assigns habitat-cell suitability across flow scenarios in the Baltic config. The NMCM habitat classification aligns closely with InSTREAM habitat-cell categories (pools, riffles, etc.). Provides a methodological template for a high-resolution Baltic river habitat-cell layer. *Baltic relevance: medium (Tana, subarctic; Atlantic salmon; methodology transferable). Touches: habitat-cell suitability, flow scenario parameterization.*

**[P4 — contextual, not actionable]** Dodd, J. A., Moore, I. E., Bowman, A. W., et al. (2026). *Scale Dependent Temporal and Spatial Patterns of Population Change in Anadromous Sea Trout (*Salmo trutta*) in Scotland Over Seven Decades.* *Ecology of Freshwater Fish*, 35(4). DOI: 10.1111/eff.70064  
> 72-year rod-catch analysis showing ~50% national sea trout decline but highly variable district-level trends (Portfolio Effect). No IBM content; contextual for sea trout stock assessment. Not actionable for instream-py.

**[P5 — conceptual/predation methodology]** Scherer, A. E., Cordner, E., Brown, R. A., et al. (2026). *Stayin' alive: Strategies to reduce predation on target species are context dependent.* *Ecosphere*, 17(8). DOI: 10.1002/ecs2.70731 (OA, cc-by)  
> Chinook salmon (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) predation tethering assays in California's Central Valley. Counterintuitively, large woody material increased predation rates in some conditions. Context-dependent predation–habitat interaction. Baltic relevance low; predation sub-model methodology interest only.

## Implications for inSTREAM

1. **Baltic validation must account for M74 externally.** Tamario et al. (2026) confirm that M74 thiamine deficiency independently depresses Baltic salmon wild recruitment, with a stronger effect at longer migration distances. Because the freshwater IBM does not model the marine nutritional causes of M74, its impact on egg and early-juvenile survival is an external input — not a parameter the IBM can calibrate. When building validation targets for the Baltic baseline config (issue #1), the development team should cross-reference WGBAST smolt production estimates with published M74 incidence records (available for the same eight Swedish Baltic rivers) and avoid tuning freshwater survival rates against high-M74 years.

2. **Post-smolt survival targets from ICES.** WGBAST reports ~3 million wild smolts + 7.7–20% post-smolt sea survival. These numbers, combined with river-specific M74 status (Tamario et al.), provide a cleaner Baltic baseline survival envelope than raw recruitment data: the IBM should be targeting the smolt-production end-point (not adult return), with sea survival treated as a separate layer.

3. **Lennox et al.'s bottleneck-predation and diel depth findings** are useful for any future Curonian Lagoon marine-phase extension but are out of scope for the current freshwater IBM.

4. **Kärkkäinen et al.'s NMCM / ALS approach** is the most methodologically advanced publicly available framework for multi-discharge, large-scale mesohabitat classification for Atlantic salmon. It is directly applicable when preparing the Baltic config's habitat-cell layer (issue #1 context).

### Medium

**M1. Document and implement M74 thiamine-deficiency status as an external covariate in the Baltic baseline validation pipeline**  
*Motivated by:* Tamario et al. 2026 (10.1098/rsbl.2026.0219)  
*Effort:* Low  
The paper establishes a significant negative association between M74 incidence and wild Baltic salmon recruitment (30+ years, eight rivers). For instream-py's Baltic baseline validation (issue #1), this means that year-specific WGBAST smolt production data used as calibration targets should be annotated with M74 status (Low / Medium / High / Severe, as reported annually by Swedish authorities). Years with Severe M74 should be excluded or down-weighted from freshwater-survival calibration. Concretely: (a) add a `data/baltic/m74_annual_status.csv` table sourced from published WGBAST annexes, (b) document in the Baltic config README which calibration years are M74-affected and should be treated with caution, (c) consider a simple sensitivity check running the IBM calibration with and without high-M74 years to quantify sensitivity of the parameter estimates to this exclusion. This is a data-documentation and analysis task, not a code change.

### Low

**L1. Fjord/estuary predation bottleneck — future Curonian Lagoon marine-phase extension**  
*Motivated by:* Lennox et al. 2026 (10.1111/eff.70062)  
*Effort:* High  
The Lennox et al. data on river-mouth and bridge-bottleneck predation and diel depth use would anchor empirical parameters for a future Curonian Lagoon marine-phase model. Not actionable until a marine-phase extension is scoped; no issue opened.

**L2. NMCM multi-discharge mesohabitat classification approach — Baltic habitat-cell layer**  
*Motivated by:* Kärkkäinen et al. 2026 (10.1002/eco.70268)  
*Effort:* High  
The ALS + 2D hydraulic + NMCM methodology provides a template for a high-resolution, multi-discharge habitat-cell map of the target Baltic river reach. Useful for issue #1 but requires dedicated field data collection. Noted for future scoping; no issue opened.
